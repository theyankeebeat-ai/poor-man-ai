# Biology

Intro textbook biology for how living things are organized, divide, capture energy, pass on traits, and change by natural selection.

## Method

1. Name the level in view, from molecule to organism, population, or ecosystem.
2. Say whether the question is structure, a process, or change across generations.
3. For division, use mitosis when the chromosome number stays the same and meiosis when gametes get half that number.
4. For energy capture, write the overall photosynthesis equation and place producers in the food web.
5. For heredity, pair DNA bases A with T and G with C, then follow the alleles.
6. For populations, explain natural selection as heritable variation plus unequal survival and reproduction.
7. Tie the answer to cell theory: living things are made of cells, the cell is the basic unit of life, and new cells come from existing cells.

## Formulas

- Photosynthesis: `6 CO2 + 6 H2O → C6H12O6 + 6 O2`. This overall equation shows carbon dioxide and water becoming glucose and oxygen.
- DNA base pairing: `A-T and G-C`. Adenine pairs with thymine, and guanine pairs with cytosine, in the DNA double helix.
- Cellular respiration: `C6H12O6 + 6 O2 → 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + energy`. This overall equation releases usable energy as glucose and oxygen become carbon dioxide and water.
- Hardy-Weinberg: `p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1`. For two alleles in a non-evolving population, p and q are allele frequencies and p + q = 1.

## Checks

- Mitosis keeps the chromosome number; meiosis halves it in gametes.
- Photosynthesis is balanced as six CO2, six H2O, one C6H12O6, and six O2.
- Allowed DNA pairs are A-T and G-C, not A-G or T-C.
- Natural selection needs heritable variation and differential reproduction, not a wish to improve.

## Limits

- Intro textbook level only; it is not a lab manual or a diagnosis.
- No pathogen cultivation, no gain-of-function, no toxin production, and no protocol for growing anything hazardous.
- It states ideas and overall equations only, with no growth conditions, doses, or bench steps.
