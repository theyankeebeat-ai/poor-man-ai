# Critical thinking

**Scope:** A study card for judging a claim by its evidence, assumptions, alternatives, and named fallacies.

## Method

1. Restate the claim in neutral words.
2. List the evidence that was actually offered.
3. Name the assumption the argument needs.
4. Give one serious alternative.
5. Name a fallacy only if the reasoning matches it.
6. State the conclusion strength and what evidence is still missing.

## Formulas

- **Claim:** The statement being asserted. Restate it so the maker would recognize it.
- **Evidence:** The observation or source offered for the claim. Keep evidence separate from the conclusion drawn from it.
- **Assumption:** An unstated idea the argument needs in order to work. Name it explicitly before judging the conclusion.
- **Alternative:** Another explanation that also fits the evidence. A live alternative lowers confidence even when the original claim is possible.
- **Ad hominem:** Attacking the person instead of the claim. An insult is not a rebuttal, though a relevant conflict of interest can still bear on the source.
- **Straw man:** Misrepresenting a position, then attacking that misrepresentation. Compare the restatement with what was actually said.
- **False dilemma:** Treating two options as the only options. Ask what other choices or mixed positions were left out.
- **Hasty generalization:** Drawing a broad rule from too few or unrepresentative cases. Ask how many cases were seen and whether they were selected fairly.
- **Appeal to authority:** Treating a citation as proof when expertise or evidence is missing. A relevant expert is a lead, not a substitute for the evidence itself.
- **Correlation is not causation:** Two things varying together do not by themselves show that one causes the other. A correlation can suggest a hypothesis; it does not identify the cause.

## Checks

- The restated claim matches what was actually said.
- Evidence is listed separately from the conclusion.
- The alternative could still fit the same evidence.
- A named fallacy matches its definition, not merely a disliked conclusion.

## Limits

- Naming a fallacy does not prove the conclusion is false.
- This card does not replace evidence from the subject itself.
- Conclusion strength judges the argument, not the person.
