# Chemistry

Intro chemistry for conservation of mass, molar mass, bond type, the ideal gas law, and the pH scale.

## Method

1. List the knowns, the unknown, and the units before any arithmetic.
2. Use conservation of mass: each element has the same atom count on both sides of a balanced equation.
3. Balance by inspection, changing coefficients only, then recheck the counts without changing subscripts.
4. Read molar mass from the periodic table and convert mass to moles with n = m / M for stoichiometry.
5. For an ideal gas, use PV = nRT with R = 0.0821 L·atm/(mol·K) and temperature in kelvin.
6. Call the bond an ionic bond when electrons are transferred, and covalent when electrons are shared.
7. On the pH scale, compute pH from the hydronium ion and read a lower pH as a higher hydronium concentration.

## Formulas

- Conservation of mass: `mass of reactants = mass of products`. In a closed change, mass is conserved because atoms are rearranged rather than created or destroyed.
- Moles from molar mass: `n = m / M`. Amount in moles equals sample mass divided by molar mass in grams per mole.
- Ideal gas law: `PV = nRT`. With R = 0.0821 L·atm/(mol·K), the law relates pressure in atmospheres, volume in liters, moles, and kelvin temperature.
- pH: `pH = -log10[H3O+]`. pH is the negative base-10 logarithm of the hydronium ion concentration on the pH scale.

## Checks

- Coefficients may change when balancing; subscripts and formulas of substances may not.
- Molar mass comes from the periodic table, and the mole conversion is n = m / M.
- The stated R requires atmospheres, liters, moles, and kelvin, not degrees Celsius.
- An ionic bond is electron transfer, a covalent bond is electron sharing, and a smaller pH means a larger [H3O+].

## Limits

- This card does not give a procedure to make a substance.
- It gives no preparation steps and no concentrations or recipes for drugs, explosives, poisons, or weapons.
- The gas law and pH equation only interpret data you already have; they are not a lab clearance.
