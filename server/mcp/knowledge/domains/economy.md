# Economy

Intro economics for gross domestic product, inflation as a rising price level, opportunity cost, and the direction of supply and demand.

## Method

1. Say whether you need a definition, an identity, or the direction of a change.
2. For output, expand GDP into consumption, investment, government purchases, and net exports.
3. Treat inflation as a sustained rise in the general price level, using a price index rather than one good.
4. State opportunity cost as the single next-best alternative given up by a choice.
5. For supply and demand, name which curve shifts and hold the other curve fixed.
6. Report how equilibrium price and quantity move, and do not invent a numerical price.
7. Call the result a textbook relation, not advice about what anyone should do.

## Formulas

- Expenditure GDP: `GDP = C + I + G + NX`. Gross domestic product on the expenditure side adds consumption, investment, government purchases, and net exports.
- Net exports: `NX = X - M`. Net exports equal exports minus imports and are the NX term in GDP.
- Inflation rate: `(P_t - P_{t-1}) / P_{t-1}`. The inflation rate is the fractional rise in a general price index over one period.
- Opportunity cost: `cost = value of the next best alternative given up`. A choice's opportunity cost is the single best thing not chosen, not the sum of every rejected option.

## Checks

- A GDP sum is incomplete until C, I, G, and NX are all accounted for.
- One product costing more is not, by itself, inflation of the general price level.
- Higher demand with supply fixed raises equilibrium price and quantity; higher supply with demand fixed lowers equilibrium price and raises quantity.
- Opportunity cost is the next-best alternative forgone, not every other option added together.

## Limits

- This is not investment advice and not a forecast.
- Identities and curve directions do not say what a person, firm, or government should do.
- The card has no live statistics; a measured GDP or inflation figure needs a named data source.
