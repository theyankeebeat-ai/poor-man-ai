# Trigonometry

This card solves right and oblique triangle questions from the sides and angles already given.

## Method

1. Decide from the given data whether the triangle is right or oblique.
2. Use opposite, adjacent, and hypotenuse only for a right triangle and a named acute angle.
3. Use SOHCAHTOA or a^2+b^2=c^2 only when a right angle is given.
4. Otherwise pair each side with its opposite angle in the law of sines or the law of cosines.
5. For three side lengths, set P = a+b+c and s = P/2, then apply Heron's formula.
6. If a side or angle is missing, name it; do not invent a length.

## Formulas

- **SOHCAHTOA**: `sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse, tan θ = opposite/adjacent` — In a right triangle these ratios relate one acute angle to two sides.
- **Pythagorean theorem**: `a^2+b^2=c^2` — In a right triangle, c is the hypotenuse and a and b are the legs.
- **Perimeter**: `P = a+b+c` — The perimeter is the sum of the three side lengths.
- **Heron's formula**: `sqrt[s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)], s = P/2` — three side lengths give the area; the perimeter is the sum of the sides; do not treat the word surface as Earth's surface.
- **Law of cosines**: `c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab cos C` — Relates the three sides to angle C, the angle opposite side c.
- **Law of sines**: `a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C` — Pairs each side with the sine of its opposite angle.

## Checks

- The hypotenuse is the side opposite the right angle.
- The law of sines pairs each side with its opposite angle.
- The law of cosines uses angle C opposite side c.
- Heron needs three side lengths; s is half the perimeter, not a new measurement.

## Limits

- SOHCAHTOA and the Pythagorean theorem apply to right triangles only.
- The SSA case can have zero, one, or two triangles; do not pick one without a reason.
- This card does not supply a side or angle the user did not give.
