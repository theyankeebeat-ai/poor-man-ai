# Vocabulary

**Scope:** A small study card for explaining a word in context and choosing one for an audience, not a full dictionary.

## Method

1. Read the word inside its sentence, not alone.
2. State the denotation, the literal meaning.
3. Note the connotation: positive, negative, or neutral.
4. Test a synonym and an antonym against that sentence.
5. Use context clues such as definition, example, contrast, or cause.
6. Choose the word that fits the audience and the purpose.

## Formulas

- **Synonym:** A synonym has a similar meaning; it is not always interchangeable. Swap it in only if grammar, denotation, and tone still hold.
- **Near synonym:** A near synonym overlaps in meaning but can differ in strength or use. Prefer the near synonym that matches the exact sense in this sentence.
- **Antonym:** An antonym has an opposite or clearly contrasting meaning. Use the contrast to test whether the original sense was understood.
- **Denotation:** Denotation is the literal meaning, apart from feeling. State this before discussing tone or persuasion.
- **Connotation:** Connotation is the feeling or association beyond the denotation. Call it positive, negative, or neutral for this audience, not for all time.
- **Context clues:** A nearby definition, example, contrast, or cause can narrow a meaning. Check the guess against the rest of the sentence before treating it as known.
- **Word choice:** Pick the word whose meaning and tone fit the audience and purpose. A plainer or more formal near synonym is better when the first choice would mislead that reader.

## Checks

- The synonym still fits the grammar and the tone of the sentence.
- Denotation and connotation are stated separately.
- A meaning guessed from context clues still fits the whole sentence.
- The word choice matches the stated audience and purpose.

## Limits

- This card is not a full dictionary and does not list every sense of a word.
- A synonym or near synonym is not automatically interchangeable.
- Connotation can change with the community and the context.
